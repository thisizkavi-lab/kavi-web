---
title: "The Idea of Self"
date: 2026-11-23
---

I dislike dividing the self into parts, yet it seems to move as three coupled things: body, mind, and spirit. Neglect one and the feedback loop eventually reaches the rest. Biology is simple and hard to argue with once you strip away the optimization noise. Food, movement, and rest are enough to keep the vessel working so the mind stays clear. The mind holds working memory, identity, and the craft baked into you, ready when you need it, though it also manufactures static and loops even when everything else is in order. Telling useful thought apart from background noise is half the work.

Spirit is less mysterious than people make it sound. To me, it is the feeling of moving in the right direction, the kind that creates a genuine excitement to wake up and work toward something. Nothing in life is certain, yet with that pull even pain makes sense, and without it both body and mind slowly wear down. Whether a soul exists or not, the idea works. Direction rarely comes from arguing with yourself or waiting to feel clear. It surfaces through friction, reflection, and staying with hard work long enough to see what actually matters.

None of this happens in isolation. We live among other selves, most of them never framing life this way, and identity keeps getting reshaped in the back-and-forth between us. Other people are also what keep the mind from drifting into its own echo chamber. That is why I would rather hold principles than rigid laws. A late dinner, a drink with a friend, or a flight that costs a night of sleep are just part of being human. Knowing what keeps the body well and holding a direction should never become a reason to wall yourself off. Seeing yourself and others clearly, without growing arrogant about what you see and without losing yourself trying to bend to everyone, stays paradoxical. Life is messy. You do not settle it once; you keep navigating, living until death.
